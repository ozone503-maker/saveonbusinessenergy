How Data Centers Are Changing the Texas Grid

Texas has become a major destination for data centers. That is showing up in ERCOT’s large-load interconnection queue. The numbers are large. Most of them are still requests, not operating load.

Key stat: As of mid-2026, ERCOT was tracking roughly 438–474 GW of large-load interconnection requests, with about 87–90% attributed to data centers. That is several times the grid’s recent record peak demand of roughly 85–91 GW.
Sources: ERCOT reports and board updates cited in Utility Dive, MEPBIDS, and related coverage (2026).

What the queue is (and is not)

A large-load request is a formal request to study and potentially connect a big electricity user (typically 75 MW or more under current ERCOT processes). It is not a guarantee the project will be built or energized. ERCOT and the PUCT have noted that many projects in the queue lack completed studies or face other hurdles. Only a small fraction of the requested capacity is currently energized.

Batch Zero and the 2026 audit

In 2026 ERCOT moved to a batch study process for large loads. Governor Abbott directed an audit of data center projects in the interconnection process. ERCOT paused certain energization approvals for large data center and crypto loads while verification and community impact reviews proceed. Reports to the PUCT have been targeted for December 2026.

What this means for planning

The growth is real enough to affect transmission planning and long-term forecasts. It is also uncertain enough that ERCOT has described some high load projections as preliminary and higher than expected future growth. For Texas businesses, the practical takeaway is that the grid environment is changing, and reviewing your own rate and contract timing is a concrete step you can control.

FAQ

Is all that requested power going to be built?

No. Industry and ERCOT analysis consistently treat the full queue as far larger than what is likely to materialize. Many projects are early-stage or lack studies.

Where are most requests located?

Public summaries have shown significant requested capacity in the West load zone, along with South, North, and other areas.
