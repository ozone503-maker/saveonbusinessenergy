Power factor: the hidden line on your bill

Some businesses pay extra every month and never see why. The reason is often one line on the bill: a power factor adjustment. Here is what it is and where it hides.

Power factor is how efficiently your equipment uses the power it draws — and when it drops too low, your utility can charge you for it.

What power factor is

Useful power vs. drawn power

Your equipment draws power from the grid. Not all of it does useful work. Power factor is the share that does.

A power factor of 100% would mean every bit drawn is used. Real equipment is never perfect. The question is how far below perfect it runs.

What drags it down

Motors drag it down. So do compressors, pumps, and older equipment. Anything with a big motor or a magnetic coil pulls more power than it turns into work.

That is normal for a working business. The problem starts when the number drops low enough for the tariff to notice.

The 95% line

Oncor's rule

Some utilities adjust billing demand when power factor is low. In Oncor territory, the tariff allows an adjustment when power factor falls below 95% lagging.

Source: Oncor Tariff for Retail Delivery Service, as summarized by Wire & Watt.

Where it shows on your bill

Look for a power factor line or a billing demand adjustment. It usually sits in the delivery section, not the energy section.

If it is there, it is raising your delivery bill every month. If it is not there, your power factor is fine or your tariff does not apply one.

What to do about it

  • Check your bill for a power factor line or adjustment.
  • A review checks whether it is quietly raising your bill.
  • Correction equipment exists. An electrician can tell you whether it fits your building.

FAQ

Does every business pay a power factor penalty?

No. It only applies when power factor is low and the tariff allows an adjustment. Many businesses never see the line at all.

Can a better energy rate fix it?

No. Power factor sits on the delivery side of the bill. A better energy rate helps the energy part.

The power factor adjustment needs its own look.
