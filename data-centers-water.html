Water and Cooling: The Other Data Center Resource Question

Electricity gets the headlines. Water is the other resource that local communities and regulators watch. Cooling design decides how much water a data center uses on site.

Key distinction: Evaporative cooling towers use large volumes of water (often measured in liters per kWh of IT load). Closed-loop liquid or air-cooled designs use far less on-site water — sometimes only domestic amounts for restrooms.
Sources: Industry WUE definitions (The Green Grid); Texas project announcements emphasizing closed-loop or air cooling (2025–2026); PUCT/TWDB survey reporting on existing facilities.

Trade-offs

Evaporative systems can be more energy-efficient in some climates because evaporation rejects heat effectively. Dry or closed-loop systems use less water but may require more electricity for fans or chillers. In water-stressed parts of Texas, the preference is shifting toward low-water designs. Proposed state measures have also linked grid interconnection and incentives to low-water cooling.

What is known about Texas facilities

Public surveys and company statements show a mix. Some operating campuses still use evaporative cooling. Many newer announcements highlight closed-loop liquid cooling or air cooling specifically to minimize water draw. Exact statewide totals are hard to compile because not every project reports the same way.

Practical note for businesses

Your commercial electricity bill does not list data-center water use. What it does list is your rate, demand charges, and contract end date. Those are the numbers you can act on today.

FAQ

Do all data centers use a lot of water?

No. It depends on the cooling system. Closed-loop and air-cooled designs can use very little.

Is water use regulated?

Yes. Projects need water rights or municipal supplies, and state reviews (including the 2026 ERCOT/PUCT process) ask about water sources and cooling technology.
