What Makes a Data Center Efficient?

Efficiency is measured in plain numbers. The main one is PUE — Power Usage Effectiveness. It tells you how much extra electricity the building uses beyond the servers themselves.

Key metric: PUE = total facility energy ÷ IT equipment energy. A PUE of 1.0 means every kilowatt goes to the computers. Industry averages have been around 1.4; efficient designs target 1.1–1.2.
Sources: The Green Grid definitions; U.S. data center analyses (e.g., Shehabi et al. / LBNL-style reporting, 2023–2024 figures).

Cooling is the big lever

Most overhead comes from cooling. Older designs use chillers and evaporative towers. Newer ones use direct-to-chip liquid cooling, warm-water loops (often 32–45°C), and free cooling when outdoor air is cool enough. In much of Texas, free cooling hours are limited, so liquid cooling and higher allowable temperatures matter more.

Closed-loop liquid systems can keep on-site water use very low. Air-cooled or dry-cooler designs use almost no water for cooling but may use a bit more electricity. Many Texas projects now advertise closed-loop or air-cooled approaches specifically to limit water draw.

Heat reuse

When return water leaves the servers warm enough (around 40–45°C), it can sometimes be used for building heat or other processes instead of being rejected. This is still uncommon but is designed into some modern campuses.

Why it matters for the grid and for businesses

A more efficient data center needs less generation and transmission capacity for the same computing work. For ordinary Texas businesses, the parallel is straightforward: the more efficient your own usage and contract, the less you are exposed to rising wholesale or delivery costs.

FAQ

Is PUE the only efficiency number?

No. Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) tracks liters of water per kWh of IT load. Both matter in Texas.

Do efficient data centers still use a lot of power?

Yes. Even at PUE 1.2, a 100 MW IT load still needs roughly 120 MW of total power. Efficiency reduces the multiplier, not the base load.
