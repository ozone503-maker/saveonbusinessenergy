Solar and storage for a Texas business: does it pencil out?

Solar panels on a Texas roof make sense on paper. Texas has sun, and businesses use power in the daytime. Whether it pencils out for your business depends on three things: your usage, your roof, and a federal tax credit with a deadline.

Solar cuts the power you buy. Storage moves it to the hours it costs most. Whether it pencils out depends on your usage, your roof, and a tax credit with a deadline.
Key stat: The federal Clean Electricity Investment Credit under Section 48E is worth up to 30% of a qualifying commercial solar project's cost. New projects that begin construction after July 4, 2026 must be placed in service by December 31, 2027, or the credit drops to zero.
Source: 26 U.S.C. § 48E; One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21); IRS Notice 2025-42. Energy storage is not subject to the solar placed-in-service cutoff.

What solar does

Daytime power, from your roof

Solar makes power while the sun is up. That is when most businesses use the most power. Every kilowatt-hour your panels make is one you do not buy.

What it doesn't do

Solar does not replace your electricity contract. You still need a provider for the power you use at night, on cloudy days, and beyond what your panels make.

It also does not change your TDU delivery charges. The wires still deliver what you use from the grid.

What storage adds

Use it when it costs most

A battery stores daytime solar and lets you use it later. That matters because the most expensive power is often the late-afternoon peak, when cooling and equipment run hardest.

Storage can also smooth your demand peak. A lower peak can mean a lower demand charge.

The money question

The federal credit — and its deadline

The federal credit under Section 48E is worth up to 30% of a qualifying commercial solar project's cost. Systems under 1 MW AC get the 30% rate automatically.

The deadline matters now. Projects that begin construction after July 4, 2026 must be placed in service by December 31, 2027, or the credit is zero. Check the current rules with a tax advisor before you count on it.

What decides payback

  • How much power you use in the daytime.
  • Your roof — size, direction, and condition.
  • Your current rate and contract.
  • The incentives in place when you build.

Before you sign anything

  • Get the quote in writing, with the full cost and the expected production.
  • Check the credit deadline with a tax advisor.
  • Compare it against a contract review first. A better rate may be the cheaper step.

FAQ

Does solar replace my electricity contract?

No. You still need a provider for the power your panels do not make.

Solar cuts what you buy. It does not end the contract.

Is the tax credit still available?

Yes, under Section 48E, up to 30% for a qualifying commercial project. But new projects face the December 31, 2027 placed-in-service deadline. Check with a tax advisor for your situation.

Thinking about solar? Check your rate first.
