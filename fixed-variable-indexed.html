Fixed vs. variable vs. indexed: which rate fits your business?

Most commercial contracts offer one of three rate types. They differ in one thing: how much surprise your bill can hold. Here is each one, plainly.

Fixed is a known number. Variable moves with the market. Indexed moves with an index. The right one depends on how much surprise your business can handle.

The three rates

Fixed

A fixed-rate contract keeps the energy rate level through the term. A fixed rate gives you a known number for the term.

Your bill can still change. Usage, demand charges, delivery charges, and taxes move on their own. The rate does not.

Variable

A variable rate can change with the market. It can move up or down, usually month to month.

That means no surprises are locked in, and no savings are locked in either. The rate follows the market wherever it goes.

Indexed

An indexed rate is tied to a published index, usually a wholesale market price. The rate moves when the index moves.

It is like a variable rate with a formula you can see. When market prices are low, an indexed rate can be low. When they spike, it spikes with them.

How to choose

  • Can your business handle a higher bill some months, or do you need a number you can budget?
  • How long is the term, and what happens when it ends?
  • What does the contract say about changing rate types early?

The trap in all three

The end date

After a fixed contract expires, many businesses move to a month-to-month or holdover rate if they have not signed a new contract. Check your contract, because the new rate can be much higher.

That trap is not about the rate type. It is about the end date. Know it before it arrives.

FAQ

Can I switch rate types mid-contract?

Most contracts lock the rate type for the term. Changing early usually means a termination fee. Check your contract before you assume you can switch.

Is indexed ever cheaper?

It can be when market prices are low. It can also spike when they do.

That is the tradeoff. An indexed rate does not cap itself.
