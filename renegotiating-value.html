The value of renegotiating your electricity contract

A commercial electricity contract has an end date. When the fixed term ends, many businesses roll onto a much higher month-to-month or holdover rate. Renegotiating before that happens is how you avoid paying the holdover rate.

The cheapest time to renegotiate is before your fixed term ends. After that, you are already paying the holdover rate.

What renegotiating actually means

More than the headline rate

Renegotiating means comparing what you pay now against what providers are offering now, for how your business actually uses power. It is not just the headline rate.

The contract language matters as much as the number. Hidden terms can cost you even when the rate looks good on paper.

The cost of waiting

The holdover rate

After a fixed contract expires, many businesses move to a month-to-month or holdover rate if they have not signed a new contract. Check your contract, because the new rate can be much higher.

The time to compare is before the end date, not after the higher rate has already started.

Demand charges

For many commercial meters, peak demand (kW), not just kilowatt-hours, is a large part of the delivery bill. That is separate from the energy rate a provider quotes.

Power factor

Some utilities adjust billing demand when power factor is low. In Oncor territory, the tariff allows an adjustment when power factor falls below 95% lagging. We check whether this is quietly raising your bill.

Source: Oncor Tariff for Retail Delivery Service, as summarized by Wire & Watt.

Tax status

Texas generally charges sales tax on business electricity, with exemptions in specific cases. We look for exemptions your business may be missing.

FAQ

When should I start renegotiating?

Before the fixed term ends, not after. Once you are on a holdover rate, you are already paying it. Start comparing while you still have a contract in place.

Does renegotiating mean switching providers?

Not necessarily. It means comparing what is available now against what you are paying.

A broker can show you offers from more than one provider. You decide.
