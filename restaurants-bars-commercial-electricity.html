HVAC, refrigeration, and the kitchen

Coolers, freezers, ovens, dishwashers, and rooftop units do not take turns. When they run together, demand on the meter rises even if the monthly kilowatt-hours look familiar. That demand charge sits on the delivery side of many commercial bills. It is separate from the energy rate a supplier quotes.

Late hours make the pattern worse. The equipment does not clock out when the last table does.

A fixed energy rate is easier to budget

A fixed-rate supply contract keeps the energy price level through the term. It does not freeze delivery charges, and it does not stop a hot month from using more power. It does make the supply portion of the bill less of a surprise than a rate that moves with the market, or a holdover rate after a contract expires.

Questions from restaurants and bars

Why do restaurant and bar electric bills jump?

Kitchens, refrigeration, and air conditioning run for long hours, and they often peak at the same time. The bill can also jump when a fixed contract ends and the account moves to a month-to-month or holdover rate. A review reads the bill instead of guessing which piece moved.

Does a lower energy rate remove delivery charges?

No. The retail electric provider supplies the energy. The local utility still delivers it and still bills delivery charges, including demand on many commercial meters. Compare the energy rate and the delivery charges together.

Can a restaurant still under contract start a review?

Yes. You can send a bill and compare options before the end date. A new contract usually starts when the current term ends. Check any early-exit language before you sign.

Is a fee billed to the restaurant or bar?

No fee is billed to your business for this service. Brokerage services are provided through USA Energy Partners. Brokers are typically paid by the retail electric provider through a commission included in the supply rate. We are not a retail electric provider.
