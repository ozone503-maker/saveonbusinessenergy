Data Centers and Renewable Energy in Texas

Texas already has large amounts of wind and solar. Data centers are both customers of that power and, in some cases, developers of their own generation and storage.

Context: ERCOT has substantial wind and solar capacity. Large data center projects increasingly announce co-located gas, solar, storage, or hybrid arrangements rather than relying solely on the grid.
Sources: ERCOT generation mix reports; company announcements for projects such as Google/Intersect (Meitner) and various West Texas campuses (2025–2026).

Common power arrangements

  • Grid power with renewable contracts or RECs.
  • Behind-the-meter gas generation (often for reliability or speed).
  • Co-located solar + battery with gas firming.
  • Hybrid campuses that combine on-site generation and grid interconnection.

Many of the largest announced Texas projects emphasize on-site or dedicated generation precisely because the interconnection queue for pure grid service is long and currently subject to additional review.

What this means for ordinary businesses

More generation and storage on the system can help reliability over time. It does not automatically lower the rate on your commercial bill. Your rate depends on your provider contract, TDU charges, demand, and timing. The practical step remains reviewing your own agreement.

FAQ

Do data centers buy all the wind and solar?

They are large customers, but ERCOT’s renewable fleet serves the whole system. Contracts and interconnection rules determine who gets what power when.

Can my business do something similar?

Some larger commercial customers use on-site solar or storage. Most start with a competitive rate review.
