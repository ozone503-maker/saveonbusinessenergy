Common questions about commercial electricity in Texas

Who are you?

We are independent lead generators who help Texas businesses find better commercial electricity rates. We review bills and connect businesses with options. You'll never be billed a fee for our service. We are not a retail electric provider.

Is an energy broker free? How do energy brokers get paid?

You'll never be billed a fee for our service, and a typical Texas energy broker does not send the business a separate invoice. Brokers are typically paid by the retail electric provider through a commission included in the supply rate. Texas rules require a broker to tell you how they are compensated before services begin.

What is an energy broker? Are energy brokers legit?

An energy broker does not generate or deliver electricity. A legitimate broker shops your account to multiple retail electric providers, helps you compare contracts, and is registered with the Public Utility Commission of Texas when providing brokerage services. Ask for the broker's PUCT registration number.

What is the average commercial electricity rate in Texas?

The U.S. Energy Information Administration's final 2025 average price to Texas commercial customers was 8.56 cents per kWh. These are statewide averages across every kind of commercial customer and are a useful benchmark, not a quote for your meter. They include energy and delivery.

Is electricity deregulated in Texas? What about Dallas, Fort Worth, Garland, or Denton?

In much of Texas, including most of Dallas–Fort Worth served by Oncor, businesses can choose a retail electric provider. The local utility still delivers the power. Many municipal utilities and electric cooperatives are not open to choice, including Garland and Denton. Confirm from your bill or ESI ID.

Can I use Power to Choose for my business?

Power to Choose is the official PUCT shopping site, but it is built around residential plans. For most businesses, a bill review and broker or direct-provider quotes are the practical way to compare.

How do I compare commercial electricity rates in Texas?

Start with 12 months of bills if you have them, plus one recent bill that shows your ESI ID, utility, usage, and demand. Compare offers on the same term and the same all-in picture. We can do that review for you from a bill upload.

Can I switch commercial electricity providers? Will my power go out?

If your address is in a competitive area and your current contract allows it, yes. A normal switch changes the retail electric provider only. The same utility still delivers the power, and the meter should not be disconnected.

What happens when my commercial electricity contract expires?

Service usually continues, but the negotiated fixed rate often ends. Many contracts then move the account to a month-to-month or holdover rate, which can be substantially higher. Start comparing options before the end date.

What is the difference between a fixed rate and a variable rate?

A fixed-rate plan keeps the energy rate the same through the contract term. A variable rate can move with the market. Delivery charges from your utility are separate.

Why is my business electric bill so high?

It may be the energy rate, a contract that expired, demand charges, a low power factor, sales tax that should have been exempt, or simply higher usage. Send us a recent bill and we will tell you what we see.

What do you need from me, and how fast do I hear back?

A recent bill (PDF or photo) and basic business contact information. We typically follow up within one business day with findings or next steps.
