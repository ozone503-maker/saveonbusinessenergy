What is a TDU charge, and why can't you shop it?

Your bill has two companies on it. One sells you the electricity. The other delivers it. The delivery company is your TDU, and its charges are the part of the bill you cannot shop.

The TDU charge pays for the wires, poles, and crews that deliver your power — and the state sets it, not the market.

What the TDU does

Delivery, not supply

Your transmission and distribution utility delivers the power, maintains the lines, and handles outages. It does not sell you electricity.

The retail electric provider, or REP, is the company you choose to supply your electricity in a deregulated area. The TDU is the company that gets it to your building.

Who yours is

In much of DFW, the TDU is Oncor. In some pockets, it is TNMP. Your bill names your TDU.

Why you can't shop it

Regulated, not competitive

Delivery charges are regulated. The state sets them. No provider can offer you a cheaper TDU charge, because the TDU charge does not come from the provider.

Same charge, any provider

Those delivery charges stay on your bill no matter which provider you choose. Switching providers changes the energy rate. It does not change the delivery company or its charges.

What you CAN change

  • Your energy rate, by comparing providers.
  • Your contract terms, including what happens when the fixed term ends.
  • Your demand charges, by managing your peak.
  • Your power factor, if a low power factor is raising your bill.
  • Your tax status, if an exemption applies to your business.

FAQ

If I switch providers, does my TDU change?

No. Your TDU is set by where your building is.

Switching providers changes who supplies your electricity. The delivery company stays the same.

Who do I call in an outage?

Your TDU. The delivery utility owns the lines and handles outages, not your retail electric provider.
