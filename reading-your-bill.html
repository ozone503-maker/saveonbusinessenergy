How to read your commercial bill, line by line

A commercial electricity bill looks complicated. It is mostly three kinds of charges, plus tax. Once you know which line is which, you can see what you can actually change.

Your bill has three kinds of charges — energy, delivery, and demand — and only one of them is the rate you shopped for.

The three parts, line by line

Energy charges

This is the electricity itself. Your usage in kilowatt-hours, times your energy rate. This is the number your provider quotes and the number a contract sets.

Delivery charges

This is the TDU part. Your transmission and distribution utility delivers the power, maintains the lines, and handles outages. Those delivery charges are regulated.

They stay on your bill no matter which provider you choose. In much of DFW, the TDU is Oncor. In some pockets, it is TNMP.

Demand charges

For many commercial meters, peak demand in kilowatts is a large part of the delivery bill. That is separate from the energy rate a provider quotes.

Demand is about your busiest moment, not your total usage. Two businesses can use the same kilowatt-hours and pay very different demand charges.

The numbers that decide your next move

Your ESI ID

Your ESI ID is the ID number for your electric service point. It is printed on your bill. Have it ready when anyone reviews your account.

Your contract end date

Your contract end date is on your bill or your contract. After a fixed contract expires, many businesses move to a month-to-month or holdover rate.

Check your contract, because the new rate can be much higher.

Your usage pattern

Look at more than one month. A single month hides the pattern. When do you use the most power? When does your demand peak?

What to check every month

  • Is the energy rate the one your contract promised?
  • Did demand spike this month, and do you know why?
  • Is there a power factor line or adjustment?
  • Is sales tax being charged, and should it be?

FAQ

Why does my bill change when my rate is fixed?

A fixed rate fixes the energy rate, not the whole bill. Your usage changes month to month.

Demand charges change with your peak. Delivery charges and taxes are separate from the rate.

Where is my contract end date?

On your bill or your contract. If you cannot find it, a review starts by finding it.
