How deregulated commercial electric works here

You choose the company that supplies the energy. Oncor, or TNMP in some pockets, owns the lines, reads the meter, and handles outages. Those delivery charges stay on the bill no matter which supplier you pick.

Retail choice for eligible Texas customers began January 1, 2002. City limits are not the same as utility territory. A Dallas address can still sit on a municipal system that does not offer retail choice. Garland Power and Light and Denton Municipal Electric are two examples. The utility name on the bill is the fact that matters.

Lock in a fixed rate ahead of renewal

When a fixed commercial term ends, many contracts move the account to a month-to-month or holdover rate. That rate can be higher than the rate you signed. Comparing options before the end date is how businesses avoid finding out after the bill arrives.

You can often start that comparison while you are still under contract. Ask for the same term and the full picture: the energy rate plus the utility delivery charges, not only the lowest advertised cents per kilowatt-hour.

Questions from DFW businesses

Is commercial electricity deregulated in Dallas and Fort Worth?

Most of Dallas, Fort Worth, Arlington, Plano, Irving, and Frisco is in Oncor territory, where a business can choose a retail electric provider. Some pockets are served by TNMP. Garland Power and Light and Denton Municipal Electric are generally not open to retail choice. Confirm the utility on your bill, not from the city name alone.

Does Oncor change if I switch electricity providers?

No. A normal switch changes the retail electric provider that supplies the energy. Oncor, or whichever transmission and distribution utility is on your bill, still delivers the power and still bills the delivery charges.

Can I lock in a commercial rate before my contract ends?

Often, yes. Many businesses start comparing offers before the end date, even while a fixed contract is still in force. The new term usually begins when the current one ends. Read your agreement before you sign, including any early-exit terms.
