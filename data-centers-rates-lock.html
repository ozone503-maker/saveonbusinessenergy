Locking In a Rate When Demand Is Rising

Large new loads do not automatically raise every commercial bill tomorrow. They do change the planning environment. The part you control is the timing of your own contract.

Practical risk: When a fixed-rate commercial contract ends, many businesses roll onto a higher month-to-month or holdover rate until they sign a new agreement. Starting the comparison before the end date avoids that step.
Common commercial market practice in ERCOT retail choice areas. Always confirm your specific contract language.

What rising demand can affect

  • Wholesale energy prices if demand outpaces new generation.
  • Transmission investment recovered through TDU charges.
  • The range of offers available when you shop.

Most of the large-load interconnection requests in ERCOT are still requests, not operating load. Only a small fraction has been approved to energize. The uncertainty is exactly why reviewing your current rate and end date is useful now rather than later.

Simple timing rule

Know your contract end date. Request a review 60–90 days before it ends (or sooner if the contract allows). Compare the fixed-rate options against what you would pay on a holdover. That comparison is concrete even if broader grid forecasts keep changing.

FAQ

Should I wait for data-center news to settle?

Waiting usually means accepting whatever rate is in effect when your fixed term ends. Reviewing options early keeps the choice in your hands.

Is a free review a commitment?

No. It shows your current rate, charges, and end date so you can decide whether to shop or stay.
