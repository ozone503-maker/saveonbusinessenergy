How to find your contract end date and ESI ID on your bill

Two numbers decide your next move. Your contract end date tells you when your rate can change. Your ESI ID tells anyone reviewing your account exactly which service point is yours. Both are printed on your bill.

Two numbers decide your next move: your contract end date and your ESI ID. Both are printed on your bill.

Your contract end date

Where it hides

Your contract end date is on your bill or your contract. On the bill, look near the contract or plan details, not the usage section.

On the contract, it is in the term section. It may be written as a date, or as a number of months from the start date.

Why it matters

After a fixed contract expires, many businesses move to a month-to-month or holdover rate if they have not signed a new contract. Check your contract, because the new rate can be much higher.

The end date is the deadline for comparing. Miss it, and you are already paying the higher rate.

Your ESI ID

What it is

Your ESI ID is the ID number for your electric service point. It is printed on your bill. Every metered service point has one.

What it tells you

It confirms whether your address is open to retail choice. Confirm from your bill or ESI ID, not from the city name alone.

Many municipal utilities and electric cooperatives, including Garland and Denton, are not open to choice. Your ESI ID settles it.

Source: PUCT 2003 Scope of Competition in Electric Markets in Texas.

What to do with them

  • Check the end date first. That is your deadline.
  • Have the ESI ID ready when anyone reviews your account.
  • Do not guess from your city name whether you can choose a provider.

FAQ

I can't find my end date — what then?

It is on the contract, even when it is not on the bill. If you cannot find either, a review starts by finding it.

Is my ESI ID secret?

It is printed on your bill. Have it ready for a provider or broker who is reviewing your account. Do not post it publicly.
