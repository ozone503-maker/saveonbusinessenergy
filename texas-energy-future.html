Texas energy in the future

Texas is adding electricity demand faster than any other grid in the country. Most of the new demand comes from large users like data centers. What happens next depends on how fast new generation and transmission get built, and on what businesses do about their own contracts in the meantime.

Demand is growing fast, but your bill is still set by your rate, your contract, and your usage. Those are things you can check today.
Key stat: The U.S. Energy Information Administration expects electricity demand in ERCOT to grow by 7% in 2025 and by 14% in 2026, as large data centers and cryptocurrency mining facilities come online.
Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration, Short-Term Energy Outlook, July 31, 2025.

Demand is growing fast

The numbers

Texans used more than 488 million megawatt-hours of electricity in 2025, about 5.7% more than in 2024. That is average annual growth of 5.0% since 2021, the fastest of any U.S. grid.

Summer peak demand rose from 71,110 MW in 2016 to 91,134 MW in 2026. ERCOT forecast a summer 2026 peak of 92,211 MW, about 8% above the August 2023 record of about 85,500 MW.

The long view

ERCOT's planning snapshot puts 2030 peak demand at about 138,944 MW. Its preliminary long-term forecast goes further, to about 367,790 MW by 2032 if every interconnection request in the queue were built.

ERCOT itself cautions that number is preliminary and may be inflated. Treat it as an upper planning scenario, not a prediction.

The grid is changing how it connects big users

Batch studies and SB6

In 2026 ERCOT moved to a batch study process for large loads. Texas Senate Bill 6, signed into law in June 2025, set new interconnection requirements for large loads.

The law gave ERCOT authority to curtail large-load consumption before and during declared grid emergencies. ERCOT paused certain energization approvals for large data center and crypto loads while verification and community impact reviews proceed.

Tighter margins

ERCOT's own December 2025 planning report shows reserve margins shrinking year by year through 2030 in its base scenario. That is a planning signal, not a blackout forecast.

It means the grid needs new generation and transmission to keep up with the load that is coming.

What it means for a Texas business

Pressure, not a bill

Demand growth puts pressure on planning and on rates over time. It does not set your bill. Your bill is set by your rate, your contract, your demand charges, and your usage.

The step you control

  • Know your rate.
  • Know your contract end date.
  • Review your options before the fixed term ends, not after you have rolled onto a holdover rate.

FAQ

Will my business electricity rate definitely go up?

No. Demand growth is one pressure among many, including fuel prices, transmission costs, and your own usage.

What is certain is that a contract left to roll onto a holdover rate usually costs more. Reviewing before the term ends is the step you control.

Is Texas going to run out of power?

ERCOT plans for growth every year, and its planning reports show where the tight spots are. New generation and transmission take years to build.

That is why the grid operator is changing how large loads connect. For a business, the useful question is not whether the grid will hold. It is whether your own contract is competitive.
