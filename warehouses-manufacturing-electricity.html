Electricity for warehouses and manufacturing

Warehouses, cold storage, and plants do not bill like a small shop. Load is larger, peaks matter, and the delivery utility depends on the address. We review those bills in Oncor, CenterPoint, AEP Texas, and TNMP territory and connect you with USA Energy Partners. No fee to your business. We are not a retail electric provider.

Oncor, CenterPoint, AEP Texas, and TNMP

In competitive areas, one of those utilities usually delivers the power. Oncor serves much of Dallas-Fort Worth and a large share of the state. CenterPoint serves much of the Houston area. AEP Texas and TNMP cover other competitive territories. Your bill names the utility. A city name is not enough, because municipal systems and cooperatives nearby often cannot switch suppliers.

Changing retail electric providers does not change that delivery utility. The wires, the outages, and the delivery charges stay put.

High load, cold storage, and demand

Lighting, HVAC, forklift charging, compressors, and production lines can peak together. On many commercial and industrial meters, that peak kilowatt reading is a large part of the delivery bill. It is separate from the energy rate printed in a supply offer.

Cold storage adds long compressor runtimes. A review should look at both the energy use and the demand, not only a headline cents-per-kilowatt-hour figure.

Manufacturing use and sales tax

Texas generally charges sales tax on business electricity. Electricity used predominantly in manufacturing can qualify for an exemption in specific cases. We can flag whether the bill shows tax that may deserve a closer look. We do not tell a plant it qualifies without reviewing the account. The rules sit with the Texas Comptroller, including Publication 94-124.

Questions from warehouses and plants

Which utilities serve Texas warehouses and plants?

In competitive areas the delivery utility is often Oncor, CenterPoint, AEP Texas, or TNMP. Oncor covers much of Dallas-Fort Worth and a wide part of the state. CenterPoint covers much of the Houston area. The name on your bill is the one that counts. Municipal utilities and electric cooperatives are often not open to retail choice.

Why can a warehouse bill stay high when the energy rate looks low?

Many commercial and industrial meters pay a demand charge based on peak kilowatts, not only on kilowatt-hours. Lighting, HVAC, charging, compressors, and process equipment can set that peak. A low energy rate does not remove the delivery charge.

Can cold storage be reviewed the same way?

Yes. Send a recent bill. Cold storage runs compressors for long hours, so both energy use and demand belong in the review. USA Energy Partners shops retail electric providers where the meter can choose a supplier.
