What ERCOT is

The grid operator

ERCOT stands for the Electric Reliability Council of Texas. It operates the electric grid for most of Texas, about 90% of the state's load.

It is a nonprofit, not a power company. It does not own power plants or wires. It runs the system that connects them.

Not your provider

ERCOT does not sell you electricity. Your retail electric provider does that.

Your TDU delivers it. ERCOT sits behind both of them, running the grid and the market.

What ERCOT does

  • Balances supply and demand every minute, so the lights stay on.
  • Runs the wholesale market where providers buy electricity.
  • Studies new connections — every big new user, including data centers, goes through ERCOT's interconnection queue.
  • Plans for growth, forecasting how much power Texas will need years ahead.

Why it matters to your bill

Wholesale prices

Your provider buys power in the market ERCOT runs. When wholesale prices move, provider offers move with them. Your contract locks your rate against that movement for the term.

Growth and planning

As of mid-2026, ERCOT was tracking roughly 438–474 GW of large-load interconnection requests, with about 87–90% attributed to data centers. That is several times the grid's recent record peak demand.

Most of those requests are not operating load yet. But the queue is why the grid is changing how big users connect, and why planning matters for rates over time.

FAQ

Does ERCOT set my rate?

No. Your provider and your contract set your rate. ERCOT runs the wholesale market behind it.

The market shapes what providers can offer, but the number on your contract is the number you pay.

Who do I call in an outage?

Your TDU. The delivery utility owns the lines and handles outages, not ERCOT and not your retail electric provider.
