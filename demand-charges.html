Demand charges explained

Your bill has two kinds of "how much." Kilowatt-hours are how much electricity you used. Kilowatts are how fast you used it at your busiest moment. That busiest moment is what a demand charge bills you for.

Kilowatt-hours are how much power you used. Kilowatts are how fast you used it at your busiest moment — and for many businesses, that moment sets a big part of the bill.

kW vs. kWh

Kilowatts: your peak

Kilowatts measure power at one moment. Think of it as the size of the pipe. When everything in your building runs at once, your demand peaks.

Kilowatt-hours: your total

Kilowatt-hours measure energy over time. Think of it as how much water flowed through the pipe all month. That is the number most people watch.

Both matter. The energy rate covers the kilowatt-hours. The demand charge covers the peak kilowatts.

Why demand shows up on a commercial bill

The grid builds for peaks

The grid has to be ready for your busiest moment, not your average one. Wires, transformers, and generation are sized for peaks.

For many commercial meters, peak demand is a large part of the delivery bill. That is separate from the energy rate a provider quotes.

What moves your demand charge

  • Everything running at once — air conditioning, ovens, motors, compressors.
  • A short spike counts. One bad 15 minutes can set the month.
  • Hot afternoons, when cooling and equipment peak together.

What you can do about it

Stagger the big loads

Do not start everything at the same time. Spread the big equipment across the day where you can.

Know your peak before you sign

A review looks at your demand, not just your rate. A low energy rate does not help if your demand charge is the problem.

FAQ

Can a better energy rate lower my demand charge?

No. The demand charge is on the delivery side of the bill. It is separate from the energy rate a provider quotes.

A better rate helps the energy part. Demand needs its own look.

Do homes pay demand charges?

Most homes do not see a demand charge. Residential bills are mostly about kilowatt-hours. Demand charges are a commercial-bill thing.
