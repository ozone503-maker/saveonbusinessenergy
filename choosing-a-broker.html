What to look for in a broker

An energy broker does not sell you electricity. A broker shops your business account to retail electric providers, compares offers, and helps you sign a contract. Here is how to tell a good one from a bad one.

A good broker shows you more than one provider, reads the contract language with you, and costs your business nothing for the review.

What a broker does

Three roles, three companies

  • The broker works the market for you.
  • The retail electric provider, or REP, is the company you choose to supply your electricity in a deregulated area.
  • The transmission and distribution utility, or TDU, delivers the power, maintains the lines, and handles outages. Those delivery charges are regulated and stay on your bill no matter which provider you choose.

We are independent lead generators who connect businesses with these options. We are not a retail electric provider.

Five things to check

1. Independence

A broker should shop your account to more than one provider and compare the offers side by side. If the broker only shows you one provider, you are not getting a comparison.

2. Contract language, not just the rate

Hidden terms can cost you even when the rate looks good on paper. A good broker reads the contract language with you, including what happens when the fixed term ends.

3. The whole bill, not just the energy rate

The energy rate is one part of the bill. Demand charges, power factor, and tax status can matter as much as the rate. Ask whether the broker looks at all of them.

4. What it costs your business

Ask what the review costs you. Ours costs the business nothing. No fee is billed to your business for the review.

5. PUCT registration

Ask whether the broker is registered with the Public Utility Commission of Texas. Brokerage services on this site are provided through USA Energy Partners, a PUCT-registered broker.

Broker vs. provider

The whole distinction

A provider sells you electricity. A broker works for your business in the market. That is the whole distinction.

The provider supplies the power. The TDU delivers it. The broker compares the offers and helps you sign the contract that fits how your business actually uses power.

FAQ

Does using a broker cost my business more?

No fee is billed to your business for the review. Ask any broker the same question before you start.

Can a broker guarantee a lower rate?

No. A broker compares what providers are offering and shows you the terms. Nobody can promise a rate before the providers quote it.
