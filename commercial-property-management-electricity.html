Electricity for commercial property managers

A commercial portfolio is rarely one meter. Common areas, house meters, and vacant suites each have a bill, a utility, and a contract date. Many meters, one conversation

You should not have to call a different supplier for every building. Send the bills you want looked at. We sort rate, end date, and which transmission and distribution utility serves each meter, then USA Energy Partners shops retail electric providers for the accounts that can choose.

Accounts on a municipal utility or electric cooperative often cannot switch suppliers. Those bills still belong in the stack so you know which meters are stuck and which ones can move.

Common area, house, and vacant-suite meters

Common-area and house meters keep lights, elevators, and HVAC running for the building. A vacant suite can still have an active meter and a supply contract. Leaving that bill out of a renewal is how a quiet space keeps costing money.

Different meters can end in different months. A simple list of end dates is more useful than waiting for each notice to arrive on its own.

What does not change

Switching the retail electric provider does not move the building to a new delivery utility. Oncor, CenterPoint, AEP Texas, or TNMP, whichever name is on the bill, still delivers the power. Delivery charges stay with that utility.

Questions from property managers

Can one review cover more than one meter?

Yes. A portfolio review can include common-area meters, house meters, and suite meters. Each meter still has its own utility, usage, and contract end date, so send the bills you want compared.

What about a vacant suite that still has a meter?

A vacant suite can still carry a delivery charge and a supply contract. It is worth including that bill in the review instead of assuming an empty space has no electric cost.
